# Phoenix Phearsome Phour Challenge

The Phoenix Phearsome Phour Challenge was created by Matteo in 2019. It covers four peaks in the Phoenix Mountain Preserve: Camelback Mountain, Piestewa Peak, North Mountain, and Shaw Butte. The challenge has two official formats: the tiered summit challenge and the 41-mile ultra. This skill is the canonical reference for all challenge content, structure, and brand voice.

---

## The Four Peaks

| Peak | Key Notes |
|---|---|
| Camelback Mountain | Iconic Phoenix landmark. Echo Canyon and Cholla trail approaches. |
| Piestewa Peak | Y-shaped trail structure. Trailhead and 302 west-side approaches are distinct route options with different tier requirements. |
| North Mountain | Northern anchor of the Preserve loop. |
| Shaw Butte | Southernmost peak. Often the final or first peak depending on direction. |

---

## Format 1: Tiered Summit Challenge

Participants complete 1 to 4 summits per peak within a 24-hour window. Route complexity escalates per tier. Piestewa Peak route distinctions apply at higher tiers due to its Y-shaped trail structure.

### Tier Names and Colors

| Tier | Name | Colors |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Uno Fresco | Bone/sand + sky blue |
| Tier 2 | Dos Caliente | Terracotta + deep orange |
| Tier 3 | Tres Locos | Burnt red + charcoal |
| Tier 4 | El Cuatro | Near black + gold |
| Ultra | Phoenix Phearsome Phour 70K | Full desert palette combined |

**Naming philosophy:** Spanglish. Chosen deliberately to be accessible to out-of-town visitors while reflecting Phoenix desert culture. This is a design principle, not a quirk. Apply it consistently across all challenge communications.

**No green** is used anywhere in the color system.

---

## Format 2: The 41-Mile Ultra (Phoenix Phearsome Phour 70K)

A planned out-and-back ultra covering all four peaks. Directional options: north-to-south and south-to-north. This route has never been formally run. Full route documentation is in progress. Do not fabricate route details. When referencing the ultra, note that it is the pinnacle of the challenge and that official documentation is forthcoming.

## Brand Voice and Tone

- Desert-tough, community-first, proud of Phoenix.
- Spanglish naming is intentional and should feel natural, not forced.
- Serious about the challenge structure. Warm and welcoming toward participants of all levels.
- Never condescending toward beginners. Never soft on the difficulty of higher tiers.
- The challenge is hard. Say so.
- Humor is allowed. Keep it dry and desert-dry.
- The official challenge definition must always be distinguished clearly from unsanctioned interpretations. The website is the authoritative source.

## Logo and Visual Direction

- **Aesthetic:** 1970s disco meets Arizona state flag.
- **Flag elements:** Alternating red/gold sunburst rays, deep blue lower half, copper star.
- **Logo target:** Hybrid of Logo 5 (text legibility) and Logo 1 (compositional refinement).
- **Outstanding requirements:** All text contained inside the circle. The word "Challenge" included. Thin outer bleed ring for print production.
- **Design tools:** Gemini for artistic concept generation. Canva for badge assembly and layout control.

## What to Never Do

- Do not fabricate route details for the 41-mile ultra. It is not yet fully documented.
- Do not reference the unsanctioned FKT route as the official challenge definition.
- Do not use green in any tier color application.
- Do not drop the Spanglish naming convention or replace tier names with English-only alternatives.
- Do not use em dashes. Use periods or commas instead.
- Do not omit "Challenge" from the full challenge name in formal contexts.
- Do not position Facebook as the authoritative home for challenge documentation.
