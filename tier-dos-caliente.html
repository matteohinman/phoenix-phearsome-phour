What It Is

Four Peaks. Two Summits Each. All on the Same Day.

Dos Caliente is Tier 2 of the Phoenix Phearsome Phour Challenge. Four peaks in the Phoenix Mountain Preserve, two summits per peak, all on the same calendar day. Eight total summit hits. That is the requirement. If Uno Fresco introduced you to the desert, Dos Caliente is where it starts to introduce itself back.

Camelback Mountain. Piestewa Peak. North Mountain. Shaw Butte. Each one hit twice, in any order you choose, on foot, in the same full-sun exposed terrain with no shade, no aid, and no revision to the hours posted at each trailhead. Doubling the summits does not merely double the effort. Fatigue compounds. The second summit of any peak is harder than the first. The eighth summit of the day is harder than the second.

Dos Caliente is Tier 2. You already know what these peaks feel like once. Read the warnings section before you decide you know what twice feels like.

The Peaks

Four Mountains. Two Summits Each.

Each peak requires two confirmed summit hits, completed on the same calendar day. Peak order is your choice. The route format for each peak is defined below and is not optional. Plan your sequence around the access constraints for each individual peak, particularly Camelback's sunrise-to-sunset window and the heat advisory closures at Camelback and Piestewa. There is a real consequence for showing up at a trailhead after it closes, and twice as many summits means twice as much time management.

Camelback Mountain

2,706 ft • Two summits required • Full yoyo

Two summits required as a full yoyo traverse. Start at either Echo Canyon or Cholla Trailhead. Go up to the confirmed true summit, descend to the opposite trailhead, go back up to the confirmed true summit, and return to your starting trailhead. You cross the full mountain twice, beginning and ending at the same point. Echo Canyon opens at 5am and allows you to be moving at first light. Cholla opens at sunrise. The entire mountain closes at sunset with no exceptions. Factor both traverses and all four trail segments into your timing. The Echo Canyon summit scramble requires hands and feet. That is true on your first ascent and your second. It is especially true on the descent when fatigue is in your legs and your attention has started to drift.

Piestewa Peak

2,610 ft • Two summits required • Y-format

Two summits required via the Y-format. Start at the main east-side trailhead. Go up to the confirmed summit, descend via the west-side 302 trail to the bottom, go back up to the summit from the west side, and return down to the east-side trailhead. Both summit hits are confirmed at the same point. The Y-format means you are crossing the full ridge structure of Piestewa both ways. This is not a casual variation. The 302 west-side trail is a legitimate descent and a legitimate ascent. Piestewa closes during heat advisories from 8am to 5pm. The trailhead closes at 11pm. Plan accordingly. The upper section is sustained and rocky in both directions.

North Mountain

1,919 ft • Two summits required • Full yoyo

Two summits required as a full yoyo traverse. Start from either the north road end or the Quechan Area 7 ramada side. Go up to the confirmed summit, descend to the opposite side, go back up to the confirmed summit, and return to your starting point. You cross North Mountain twice, beginning and ending at the same trailhead. North Mountain opens at sunrise. The parking lot closes at sunset. The elevation gain is the most modest of the four peaks. Crossing it twice in a day, in sequence with the other three peaks at two summits each, is not modest. Confirm the summit, not the ridge below it, on both ascents.

Shaw Butte

2,149 ft • Two summits required

Two summits required. Go up to the confirmed summit, come back down to the parking lot, go back up to the summit, and come back down. Shaw Butte opens at sunrise. The parking lot closes at sunset. The trail is consistent and the route is unambiguous. The communication tower is a visible landmark near the top. Confirm the summit, not the saddle below the tower, on both hits. Shaw Butte tends to be quieter than Camelback and Piestewa. By the time you reach Shaw Butte at Tier 2, quiet is not what you will be feeling. The desert does not grade on how many peaks you have already done today.

Before You Go

Eight Summits. No Shade. Read This.

Dos Caliente is Tier 2. You have doubled the summit count. The Phoenix Mountain Preserve has not doubled its mercy. There is no shade on Camelback, Piestewa, North Mountain, or Shaw Butte. Not on the trails, not at the summits, not on the second approach of any peak when your legs already know what the first one cost. This section is a briefing. Read it before you go.

  • ▲ Heat and sun exposure. There is no shade on Camelback, Piestewa, North Mountain, or Shaw Butte. Not on the trails, not at the summits, not in the approach corridors between them. Eight summits means you are in full sun significantly longer than Uno Fresco. Ground temperatures on exposed rock in the Phoenix desert are not moderate even in the cool season. The water you carried for four summits is not enough for eight. Recalculate before you leave the car.
  • ▲ October through April. Not summer. Not ever. Dos Caliente is a cool-season challenge. Eight summits in the Phoenix desert in summer is not a tier. It is a medical event. The window is October through April. There is no exception for early morning starts, high fitness levels, or confidence from having done Uno Fresco in May without incident. The season is the season.
  • ▲ Trail access hours. Camelback opens at 5am via Echo Canyon and at sunrise via Cholla. It closes at sunset with no exceptions. Piestewa opens at 5am and closes at 11pm. North Mountain opens at sunrise, and its parking lot closes at sunset. Shaw Butte opens at sunrise, and its parking lot closes at sunset. Camelback and Piestewa both close during heat advisories from 8am to 5pm. Each peak has its own hours. Plan each one individually. A summit that occurs outside posted access hours does not count.
  • ▲ Cumulative fatigue. Eight summits is eight summits. The second summit of any peak is harder than the first, every time, without exception. The fifth summit of the day arrives with legs that already know what four summits feel like. The eighth is harder than the seventh in ways that are difficult to fully anticipate if you have not done it before. Fatigue affects foot placement, decision-making, and your ability to accurately assess how you are doing. Build in significantly more time than your best-case estimate.
  • ▲ Rocks and technical terrain. Camelback's summit scramble on the Echo Canyon route requires hands and feet. You will do it twice. The descent is where most injuries happen, not the ascent, and that is true on the first descent and more true on the second. Piestewa's upper section is rocky and sustained. The 302 west-side trail is a legitimate technical challenge in both directions. Pay full attention on every descent.
  • ▲ Cactus. The Phoenix Mountain Preserve is a working desert. Jumping cholla, prickly pear, and saguaro are present on every peak and every approach. The cholla does not care that you already passed it on the way up. Bring tweezers. They will be used at least twice. This is not a precaution. It is a prediction with a higher sample size than Tier 1.
  • ▲ Wildlife. Rattlesnakes, scorpions, Gila monsters, javelinas, black widows, bobcats, and chupacabras are present throughout the preserve. The chupacabra sightings remain unconfirmed. We are still not ruling anything out. Watch where you step and where you put your hands. This applies especially on descents when fatigue is highest, and doubly so on the second descent of any peak when attention has genuinely started to drift.
  • ▲ Water. Carry it. All of it. Eight summit hits across four peaks with no aid stations and no reliable on-trail water sources. The amount you would carry for four summits is insufficient. Plan for the full day, then add a margin beyond that plan. No one is handing you anything on any approach at any point in the day. Your hydration is self-managed from the moment you start to the moment you finish all eight.

Official Stance of the Phoenix Phearsome Phour Challenge

We strongly encourage you not to attempt this challenge.

Dos Caliente is Tier 2. Two summits per peak. All four peaks. Eight total summit hits, all on the same calendar day. If you have completed Uno Fresco, you know what these peaks feel like once. Dos Caliente is what they feel like when you go back. The terrain does not change. The access hours do not change. The heat, the cactus, the technical descents, and the zero shade do not change. What changes is that you have to earn every one of those eight summits in a single day, with compounding fatigue and a longer window of exposure than Tier 1 requires.

Dos Caliente is not the peak of this challenge. There are two more tiers above it. That context is offered not as encouragement but as orientation. Tier 2 is the point where the challenge begins to make itself clear. The desert stops feeling like a backdrop and starts feeling like a decision you made and have to finish.

The Phoenix Phearsome Phour Challenge, its organizer, and anyone associated with it accept zero responsibility for what happens on your Dos Caliente attempt. That includes heat, cactus, wildlife, technical terrain, access-hour violations, miscalculated water, the specific feeling of standing at the base of Camelback for the second time in a day, and any philosophical developments that occur somewhere around your sixth summit when the mission is no longer abstract.

Cool season only. Water for the full day, then more. You have been warned. Go get it.

Route Rules

What Makes It Official

  • 01 Summit all four peaks twice each, all on the same calendar day. Plan all eight summit hits within the access window for each individual peak. Each peak has its own hours. Know them.
  • 02 Peak order is your choice. All eight summits must be completed on the same calendar day. No splitting peaks across calendar days. Completing one peak's two summits on day one and another peak's on day two is not Dos Caliente.
  • 03 Camelback Mountain: two summits, full yoyo traverse. Start from Echo Canyon or Cholla Trailhead. Go up to the confirmed true summit, descend to the opposite trailhead, go back up to the confirmed true summit, and return to your starting trailhead. The full mountain is crossed twice, beginning and ending at the same point. Confirm the true summit on both ascents, not the false summit on the Echo Canyon approach. All movement on Camelback must be completed before sunset. No exceptions. Opens 5am via Echo Canyon, at sunrise via Cholla. Heat advisory closure 8am to 5pm.
  • 04 Piestewa Peak: two summits, Y-format. Start at the main east-side trailhead. Go up to the confirmed summit, descend via the west-side 302 trail to the base, go back up to the confirmed summit from the west side, return down to the east-side trailhead. Confirm the true summit on both hits. Opens 5am. Closes 11pm. Heat advisory closure 8am to 5pm. Plan both crossing directions around these windows.
  • 05 North Mountain: two summits, full yoyo traverse. Start from either the north road end or the Quechan Area 7 ramada side. Go up to the confirmed summit, descend to the opposite side, go back up to the confirmed summit, and return to your starting point. The full mountain is crossed twice, beginning and ending at the same trailhead. Confirm the summit, not the ridge below it, on both ascents. Opens at sunrise. Parking lot closes at sunset.
  • 06 Shaw Butte: two summits. Go up to the confirmed summit, back down to the parking lot, up again to the confirmed summit, back down. Confirm the true summit, not the saddle below the communication tower, on both hits. Opens at sunrise. Parking lot closes at sunset.
  • 07 Document your attempt with a GPS recording showing all eight summit hits with timestamps on the same calendar day. A public Strava activity is the standard. That recording is your official documentation of completion.
  • 08 No pacers. No external pacing or on-trail support of any kind. Transportation between trailheads is permitted. Self-carry or public water sources only for on-trail hydration and nutrition.
  • 09 All trail access hours must be respected. Each peak has its own hours. A summit that occurs outside posted access hours does not count. There are no exceptions.
  • 10 To be added to the Wall of Fame, complete all four steps: submit your attempt using the official form at phoenixphearsomephour.com, provide a link to your public Strava activity showing all eight summit hits, post it to the Phoenix Phearsome Phour Facebook group at facebook.com/groups/phoenixphearsomephour, and download your badge image to share on your social networks with the hashtag #phoenixphearsomephour. All four are required for official recognition.

What Comes Next

Dos Caliente Is Not the Top

The tier system continues from here. Tres Locos requires three summits per peak in a single day, twelve total. El Cuatro, Tier 4, requires four summits per peak, sixteen total, all on the same calendar day. Above the tier system is the Phoenix Phearsome Phour 70K, a continuous out-and-back ultra hitting all four peaks with eight total summit hits across roughly forty-four miles.

Full documentation for each tier and the 70K Ultra route is at phoenixphearsomephour.com. You have now done these four peaks twice each in a single day. The question of what three times feels like is already forming. That is what this challenge does.
